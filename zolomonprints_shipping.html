Shipping Your Flowers
for botanical scanography

Your flowers are the primary material of the work. The scan happens at peak bloom — so timing, handling, and packaging determine what the final print can be.

Before Anything Else

1

Register your shipping label before you drop off. If you purchased a prepaid ZolomonPrints shipping label, follow the link in your booking confirmation to complete registration and receive your QR code before visiting the carrier. We do not provide boxes or additional packing materials.

2

Flowers must arrive within 4 days of your event. Scans happen at peak freshness. Flowers arriving beyond this window may be too wilted to produce the quality of work you commissioned. Overnight shipping is strongly recommended.

3

Keep them in water. Do not refrigerate or freeze. Flowers should remain in fresh water at room temperature until the moment of packaging.

4

Select "no signature required" when shipping. A signature hold means your flowers sit with the carrier. We cannot be held liable for wilting caused by delivery holds you could have prevented.

Step 02 — Immediately after your event

Care before packaging

We recommend designating someone — a family member, wedding planner, or trusted friend, to manage the bouquet after your event ends. Give them this document in advance.

  • 1 Cut the stems at an angle immediately after the event. Angled cuts allow the stems to draw water efficiently. If stems are bundled tightly, make small vertical cuts along the sides of the bundle instead.
  • 2 Place in fresh water, out of direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate or freeze — this causes mold during transit and compromises petal structure in the scan.
  • 3 Handle the petals as little as possible. Fingerprints, pressure marks, and bruising are visible in high-resolution scanography. The less the petals are touched between event and studio, the better the final work.
  • 4 Do not apply any spray, preservative mist, or water directly to the petals before packaging. Residue on petals transfers to the scanner glass and affects image quality.
Step 03 — The day of shipping

Packaging & shipping

Speed matters more than perfection here. A bouquet shipped promptly in adequate packaging will always outperform a perfectly packed bouquet that arrived late.

  • 1 Soak several paper towels in cool water until saturated.
  • 2 Wrap the damp paper towels around the stems only. Do not let wet material touch any part of the flower heads or petals.
  • 3 The paper towels should be damp enough to hydrate the flowers throughout transit, not so wet that water drips freely.
  • 4 Wrap a plastic bag (a zip lock or grocery bag works well) around the outside of the paper towels to seal in moisture. No plastic should contact the flower heads — stems only.
  • 5 Do not wrap plastic around the flowers themselves. Plastic around petals creates a greenhouse effect that accelerates wilting.
  • 6 Secure the paper towel and plastic wrap with a rubber band or florist tape so the moisture layer stays in place and does not leak into the box.
  • 7 Lightly wrap the full bouquet in brown packing paper. No packing peanuts — they shift in transit and can compress petals.
  • 8 In warm months, place sealed ice packs inside a zip lock bag and include them in the shipping box to keep flowers cool in transit. Do not request these back — they will not be returned.
  • 9 Place the wrapped bouquet in your shipping box and fill remaining space with packing paper to prevent movement.
  • 10 Ship via FedEx overnight with tracking. If your event falls on a weekend, shipping the following Monday is acceptable as long as flowers have been kept in water. Do not select signature required.
  • 11 Email your tracking number and estimated delivery window to zoey@zolomonprints.com as soon as your package drops.
